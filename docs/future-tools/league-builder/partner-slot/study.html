The diagonal, revisited

The first seam cut its own halves: the lower one lost its heading and the two stopped reading in the same order. These three keep the seam and give each half a rectangle that fits entirely inside it, so nothing is clipped and both halves read heading, sprite, name — and, in the second row of each, the Pokémon's types.

What the pair has to obey

The partner is not on the team The signature picker locks to the card's own lines once the template is full — there is no seventh seat for a line from outside it. The partner picker never locks: a partner is the leader's companion, not their sixth Pokémon, so it stays open and the pair can be off-type.

It is still seated when there is room Choosing a partner while an evolution line is free places it in that line. On a five-line card the partner becomes the sixth line and the partner; only on a full card does it live in the slot alone. The same pick means two different things depending on how full the card is, and the picker has to say which before the tap.

Four states, not two Either half can be empty, so every geometry has to read at a glance in all four — and an empty half has to invite a tap without competing with the filled one beside it.

Every panel is drawn at the card's own geometry — 148 × 204 at a 390px frame, the three columns and four rows the signature slot occupies today — with the app's own sprites at the app's own pixel scale. A sprite that looks cramped here is cramped on the card.
